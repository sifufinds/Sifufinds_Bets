2. Why the AFCON opening games matter to African punters

Senegal – a new era under Vieira

Patrick Vieira’s first match in charge ended 1‑1 against Mozambique. For bettors, a debut draw can be interpreted two ways:

  1. Cautious optimism – the squad showed resilience, especially after conceding first.
  2. Uncertainty on attacking output – the lack of a win may keep Senegal’s “to‑win‑the‑tournament” odds relatively high.

Most African bookmakers, including Bet9ja and Sportybet, will likely keep Senegal to win the AFCON at premium levels for now, while offering Match‑Winner and Both Teams To Score (BTTS) markets at more balanced prices. The draw also opens a niche for First‑Goal Scorer bets – keep an eye on Sadio Mané or the emerging youngster Youssouf Moussa.

Nigeria – a comeback that fuels confidence

Nigeria’s 2‑1 triumph over Madagascar after trailing 0‑1 is classic Super Eagles drama. The comeback will probably tighten the Nigeria win odds for the next qualifier (against a tougher opponent) and boost the Over 2.5 Goals market across the group.

Betting operators love a story of resilience – expect Live‑In‑Play markets to spike when Nigeria equalises early, and Next‑Goal odds to swing in favour of the Super Eagles’ striker, often Victor Osimhen.

The knock‑on effect of Nations League form

KickOff’s note on EPL stars carrying form into the Nations League, plus Haaland’s Norway surge, signals a broader trend: when Europe’s elite perform, African bettors tend to shift focus to European match‑winner markets, especially on platforms like 1xBet that offer deep coverage of the Nations League.

For example, a Norway‑Sweden clash featuring Haaland could see Norway to win odds dip on Betway, while Both Teams To Score might rise on Bet9ja as punters anticipate a high‑scoring affair.

Why does this matter for AFCON? Many African national sides draw heavily from Europe‑based players. If a star like Haaland is in top form, his compatriots in African squads (e.g., Norway‑born players for Nigeria) may be viewed as more likely to deliver goals, nudging Goal‑Scorer markets upward.
