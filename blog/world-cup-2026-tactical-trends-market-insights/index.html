The FIFA World Cup 2026 is in full swing, and it's not just the beautiful game that's grabbing headlines – the tactical trends and transfer news are also making waves. In this article, we'll dive into the latest developments and highlight some value bets for African bookmakers.

Tactical Trends Shaping the Tournament

The Resurgence of the 4‑4‑2

After a decade of dominance by three‑point formations, the classic 4‑4‑2 is re‑emerging across several groups. Teams such as England, Argentina and the host United States have reverted to a balanced back‑four, two central midfielders and two strikers to exploit width and aerial strength.

  • Stat check: In the first ten matches of the group stage, 6 teams deployed a 4‑4‑2 at least once, accounting for 38 % of total line‑ups – a 12 % rise compared with the 2018 tournament.
  • Why it matters for bettors: The dual‑striker setup often leads to higher goal‑scoring odds. In matches where a 4‑4‑2 is confirmed, the over‑2.5 goals market has shown an average implied probability of 48 % versus 42 % for other formations.

The Persistence of the False Nine

While the 4‑4‑2 gains traction, the false nine remains a potent weapon, especially for teams with technically gifted playmakers. Brazil’s use of Vinícius Júnior dropping deep to link midfield and attack has forced defenders into uncomfortable positions, creating space for wingers like Raphinha.

  • Stat check: False‑nine deployments have produced a 0.72 x conversion rate for chances created per 90 minutes, the highest among all forward roles this tournament.
  • Betting angle: Markets for “first goal scorer” tend to overvalue traditional centre‑forwards. In Brazil’s matches, the false nine’s odds have been on average 1.8 x longer than the listed striker, presenting potential value for sharp bettors.

Full‑Backs as Secondary Playmakers

The modern full‑back has become an auxiliary playmaker. Mexico’s Jesús Gallardo and Spain’s Jordi Alba are averaging 1.6 key passes per game, rivaling central midfielders. This shift inflates the “assist” and “anytime goal” markets for full‑backs, especially when they operate in a high‑press system.

Transfer News Impacting the World Cup

Marc Cucurella’s Real Madrid Switch

Cucurella’s surprise move to Real Madrid just hours before Spain’s opening game has sent ripples through the squad. His ability to play both left‑back and wing‑back gives Spain tactical flexibility, potentially prompting a switch to a 3‑5‑2 in later rounds.

  • Market implication: Spain’s clean‑sheet odds have shortened from 2.20 to 2.00 across most African bookmakers, reflecting the defensive upgrade.

The Fernandez Question

Cucurella’s former Chelsea teammate, Alejandro Fernández, has not yet confirmed a destination. Rumours point to a late‑season loan to Barcelona, which would give the Catalans a left‑flank boost. Should the move materialise, Barcelona’s over‑2.5 goals market could tighten, as Fernández’s crossing ability historically adds 0.3 goals per match.

Ruben Amorim to AC Milan

Five months after his abrupt exit from Manchester United, Amorim’s appointment at AC Milan brings a possession‑centric philosophy to the Italian side. His preferred 4‑2‑3‑1 could see Milan dominate midfield battles in the Europa League, while also influencing the World Cup odds for Italy’s defensive solidity.
