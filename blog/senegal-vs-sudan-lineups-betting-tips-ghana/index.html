The line‑ups you need to know

Senegal typically lines up in a 4‑3‑3 formation. Expect the back four to feature a solid centre‑back pairing of Kalidou Koulibaly and Abdou Diallo, with Sadio Mané spearheading the attack on the left wing. The midfield trio often includes Idrissa Gueye, who drives the tempo, and a creative No 5 in Cheikhou Kone. Sudan, on the other hand, favours a 4‑2‑3‑1 set‑up. Their lone striker, Seif Dawoud, is supported by two wide forwards who love to stretch the defence. The midfield pivot is anchored by veteran Ali Al‑Hassan, while the full‑backs push high to create overloads.

Both sides have a few injury doubts. Senegal’s star forward Ismaïla Sarr has missed recent training sessions, and Sudan’s right‑back Mohamed Abdelrahman is listed as a possible late scratch. Keep an eye on the official team sheets 90 minutes before kick‑off – a last‑minute change can swing the odds dramatically.

Betting angles that matter

  1. First goal scorer – Mané’s record against Sudan is impressive, but Sudan’s Dawoud has a habit of finding the net early in qualifiers. The safest play is a “anytime goal scorer” market that covers both players.
  1. Both teams to score – Senegal’s defence is compact, yet Sudan’s wing play often forces the back line into errors. The BTTS market offers decent odds, especially if you pair it with a low‑minimum‑odds requirement.
  1. Half‑time/Full‑time – Senegal tends to dominate the opening 45 minutes, but Sudan can rally after the break. A half‑time/Full‑time bet of Senegal/Draw or Senegal/Senegal can lock in value.
  1. Over/Under 2.5 goals – The match historically averages 2.3 goals. With both teams capable of striking, the “over 2.5” market is worth a small stake, especially if the bookmaker offers a lower minimum odds threshold.
