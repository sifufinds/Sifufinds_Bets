On a June evening in Philadelphia, with storm clouds rolling in off the horizon, Kylian Mbappé dispatched a left-footed curler into the top corner from 25 yards and stood with his arms wide open, absorbing the moment. The goal was brilliant. It was also his 15th at a World Cup, placing him within touching distance of Miroslav Klose's all-time record of 16. He scored the 16th before the night was out, drawing level with the German legend in a 3-0 France victory over Iraq — a match so extraordinary it became the first in World Cup history to be suspended by a storm, with a lightning delay lasting over two hours at the start of the second half.

Mbappé is 27 years old. He has competed at three World Cups. He now has more World Cup goals than any French player in history, and on this evidence, he is just getting started.

Senegal, the France Record, and the Journey Here

Cast your mind back to 16 June 2026, when France began their campaign against a lively Senegal side in a match that was far tighter than many had anticipated. Mbappé scored twice as France won 3-1.

His second goal that evening — a stunning stoppage-time strike from distance — was also his 58th for France, making him the country's all-time leading international goalscorer, surpassing Olivier Giroud's long-standing record of 57. It was a milestone that had been building for years, and Mbappé reached it on the sport's biggest stage. Not a quiet friendly. A World Cup group game, under the lights, with the world watching.

He had arrived at this tournament carrying significant expectation. After his eight-goal Golden Boot performance in Qatar in 2022 and his move to Real Madrid in the summer of 2024, the question was never whether Mbappé can deliver at club level — he has done that comprehensively — but whether he can sustain the same level on the international stage across multiple tournaments.

So far: a very convincing yes.

The Iraq Match That Rewrote the Record Books

22 June 2026. Philadelphia. France v Iraq, Group I.

What should have been a routine group clincher turned into the most memorable match of the tournament's first fortnight. After 45 minutes, France led 1-0 through Mbappé's curling opener — a left-footed effort from outside the box that gave Iraq goalkeeper Ahmed Basil no chance whatsoever. Then the heavens opened.

A severe lightning storm forced the referee to suspend play at half-time, with electricity striking within range of the stadium. Players retreated. Fans sheltered under whatever cover they could find. For over two hours, one of the biggest sporting events on earth simply stopped.

When the second half finally resumed, Mbappé picked up exactly where he had left off. A mix-up between Basil and a defender left the ball rolling loose inside the box, and Mbappé was there to prod it home for his 16th World Cup goal. Klose's record, held since 2014, equalled in a moment of goalkeeping chaos.

Ousmane Dembélé added a third late on, but the evening belonged to Mbappé.

He also earned his 100th cap for France during this tournament — at just 27 years and 184 days old, becoming the youngest player in French football history to reach that landmark.

The Records He Has Already Rewritten

The depth of what Mbappé has already achieved at World Cups deserves more than a passing mention.

He has delivered multi-goal performances in six separate World Cup matches — five braces and one hat-trick — across three different tournaments. He scored in four consecutive World Cup tournaments. He is only the fourth man in history to score in three or more consecutive World Cup appearances. His total of 16 career World Cup goals, at the age of 27, puts him in a conversation that very few players have ever entered.

For context: Ronaldo (the Brazilian, widely considered among the game's greatest goalscorers) finished his World Cup career with 15. Mbappé has already surpassed that.

And yet Lionel Messi — two goals ahead on 18, and five years Mbappé's senior — provides a daily reminder that the record Mbappé is chasing keeps moving. The Golden Boot race is alive. Very much so.

Can He Win the Golden Boot?

Mbappé won the Golden Boot at the 2022 World Cup in Qatar with eight goals. He is attempting to become the first player in football history to win the award at two separate World Cups. Nobody has ever done that.

Right now, Messi leads the 2026 tournament top-scorer chart with five goals — his journey to that record is covered in full in our piece on Messi's all-time 18-goal World Cup record. Mbappé has four. The gap is a single goal.

France still have matches to play. Their group finale against Norway arrives on 26 June — Erling Haaland's Norway, who have been organised in defence and carry genuine threat on the counter. Beyond that, France are the bookmakers' favourites to win the tournament outright at around 4.90 decimal odds, which means they are expected to go deep into the latter stages. A deep run means more matches. More matches mean more opportunities for Mbappé to extend his tally and overhaul Messi.

This Golden Boot race is, right now, a genuine two-horse competition. Back Messi for consistency; back Mbappé for trajectory.
