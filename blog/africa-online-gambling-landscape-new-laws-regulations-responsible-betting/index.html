Emerging Legal Frameworks Across Africa

The push for regulatory clarity is strongest in the region’s most populous countries. South Africa’s upcoming “Integrated Gambling Act” seeks to bring online operators under the same licensing regime that governs land‑based casinos, while Nigeria’s “Betting and Gaming Act” will create a dedicated regulatory body for all forms of wagering. Kenya, by contrast, has retained a more fragmented approach, allowing the Independent Betting Commission to issue licenses for land‑based and online betting in separate categories.

These divergent paths mean that operators must adapt quickly: a platform licensed in Nigeria may still need to seek a separate South African license if it wishes to offer PSL betting. For bettors, the main takeaway is that the safety net provided by a local licence is becoming more robust, but cross‑border betting still carries regulatory risk.

FAQ

Why Offshore Casinos Remain Part of the African Gambling Landscape?

Because many African players still seek higher odds and a broader range of markets that local licensed sites may not offer, especially in countries with strict regulatory limits.

Is online gambling legal in Africa?

Yes, most African countries have legalized online gambling under a licensing regime, but the legal status varies by country and is subject to ongoing legislative changes.

Why African gambling regulation varies so widely?

Differences in colonial legal heritage, religious influence, economic development, and political priorities drive the diversity in regulatory approaches across the continent.

What are the new laws in South Africa regarding online gambling?

South Africa’s Integrated Gambling Act will unify all gambling forms under one licence, impose a 30 % tax on gross betting turnover, and create a blocklist for unlicensed operators.
