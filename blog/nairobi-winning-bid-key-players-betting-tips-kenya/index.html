The Architects of Nairobi’s Winning Bid

The Visionary Administrator – Hussein Maalim

Hussein Maalim, former president of the Athletics Kenya federation, was the public face of the bid. While recovering from a minor operation, he monitored the decision from a hospital bed, refusing to miss the moment when the World Athletics Council announced Nairobi as the official host. His relentless lobbying in Budapest, his ability to weave Kenya’s athletic heritage into a compelling narrative, and his emotional appeal – described by Lord Coe as “compelling and emotional” – were pivotal. Maalim’s experience as a former sprinter gave him credibility when he spoke about athlete welfare, training facilities and legacy projects.

The Government Liaison – Minister of Sports, Hon Peter Munya

Munya coordinated the political front. He secured cabinet backing, ensured that the bid budget aligned with Kenya’s Vision 2030 development plan and facilitated high‑level meetings with the World Athletics Council. By positioning Nairobi as a gateway to East African sport, he convinced the council that the city could deliver a world‑class event while fostering regional growth. His diplomatic outreach to neighbouring federations added weight to the proposal, turning it into a continental project rather than a solo national effort.

The Business Champion – CEO of Kenya Airways, John Muthomi

Muthomi’s role was to guarantee logistics and connectivity. He offered complimentary flight slots for athletes and officials, promised a seamless travel experience and pledged to showcase Nairobi’s modern airport as a hub for African sport. His commitment reassured the council that participants would face minimal travel hassles, a factor that often decides between competing bids. The partnership between Kenya Airways and the bid committee also unlocked sponsorship deals that bolstered the financial credibility of the project.

The Grassroots Mobiliser – Former Marathon Star, Eliud Kipchoge

Kipchoge’s involvement was symbolic but powerful. He toured schools, ran community events and used his global fame to rally public support. By sharing personal stories of training on Nairobi’s streets, he painted a vivid picture of how the city could inspire the next generation of athletes. His endorsement added an emotional layer that resonated with both the council and everyday Kenyans, turning the bid into a national pride campaign.

These four figures, each playing a distinct yet complementary role, turned Nairobi’s bid from a hopeful proposal into a guaranteed victory. Their combined influence illustrates how strategic leadership, government backing, corporate partnership and grassroots enthusiasm can win a global sporting contract.

Licensing, Regulation and Player Protection in Kenya

All bookmakers operating in Kenya must hold a licence from the Betting Control and Licensing Board (BCLB). The regulator’s mandate is to safeguard players, ensure fair play and enforce anti‑money‑laundering standards. Each operator listed displays a BCLB licence number – for example, Betika Kenya’s licence is BK0000679. This licence guarantees that the bookmaker’s software is audited, that odds are calculated transparently and that player funds are kept in segregated accounts.

Kenyan law also requires operators to implement age verification (minimum 18 years) and to provide clear information on wagering requirements. The BCLB conducts regular audits and can impose fines or revoke licences for non‑compliance. As a bettor, you should always check that the bookmaker’s licence number is displayed on the homepage or in the footer of the site.

Responsible Gambling: Play Smart, Play Safe
