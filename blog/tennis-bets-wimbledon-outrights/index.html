Why Wimbledon Still Rules the Betting Calendar

Wimbledon is the only Grand Slam still played on grass, a surface that rewards big serves, low‑bounce shots and aggressive net play. Historically, players with a strong serve‑and‑volley game have thrived – think Pete Sampras, Roger Federer and, more recently, Daniil Medvedev’s revamped grass strategy. For bettors, this means that surface‑specific statistics (first‑serve points won, break‑point conversion on grass) often outweigh overall ATP/WTA rankings when scouting value.

The 2026 edition also marks the 150th anniversary of the tournament, prompting a larger prize fund and heightened media coverage across the continent. In Nigeria, Kenya and South Africa, viewership spikes during the first two weeks, translating into higher betting volumes on local platforms such as Bet9ja and SportyBet. Understanding how the grass court nuances affect each contender can therefore be the difference between a marginal win and a costly loss.
