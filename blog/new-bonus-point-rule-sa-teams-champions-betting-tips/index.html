Introduction

The new bonus‑point rule in the CAF Champions League changes how South African teams can qualify for the knockout stage, and that shift ripples straight into the betting market. In plain terms, the rule awards an extra point for wins in the group stage that are decided after extra time, meaning a 2‑1 victory after 90 minutes plus extra time now carries more weight than a regular 90‑minute win. For punters this creates fresh value on match‑winner markets and on the emerging “bonus‑point” betting lines that several South African bookmakers have begun to offer. Below we break down the rule, show how it affects the DStv Premiership clubs in the continental competition, and compare the best local bookmakers you can use to capitalise on the new opportunities.

How the New Bonus‑Point Rule Works

The Confederation of African Football introduced the rule to encourage attacking play in tightly contested groups. Every group‑stage win now yields three points as usual, but if the win is secured after extra time the victorious side receives a fourth point. A draw remains at one point, and a loss still yields none. The rule only applies to the group stage; the knockout rounds keep the traditional single‑match elimination format.

Because extra‑time wins are rarer, the fourth point becomes a premium asset. Bookmakers that have added “bonus‑point” markets will set odds on whether a particular team will finish the group with a certain number of total points, factoring in the possibility of that extra point. For South African clubs – such as Mamelodi Sundowns, Orlando Pirates and Kaizer Chiefs – the rule can turn a narrow 2‑1 victory into a decisive edge that pushes them into the top two of the group, especially when the group is tightly packed.

Impact on SA Teams in the CAF Champions League

South African sides have traditionally been strong in the CAF Champions League, but the new rule adds a strategic layer. Teams that are adept at grinding out wins in the latter stages of a match now have a statistical advantage. For bettors, the rule creates two clear angles:

  1. Match‑Winner Betting – Odds on a team to win after extra time often carry a slightly higher payout than a standard win. When a South African side is known for late‑game stamina, backing them on the “win after extra time” market can be profitable.
  1. Group‑Stage Points Markets – Bookmakers now offer over/under bets on total points after the group stage, with a special line for “bonus points earned”. If a club has a history of scoring late winners, the over‑bonus‑point line becomes attractive.

The rule also influences the betting volume on the first leg of a two‑leg tie. Punters may hedge by taking a small stake on the “bonus‑point” outcome and a larger stake on the outright winner, balancing risk across the two possible point scenarios.
