Regulatory Shifts in the United States

Recently, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposed rule changes that would eliminate the quarterly reporting requirement for publicly traded companies. While the proposal is aimed at reducing administrative burdens, its downstream impact on the iGaming sector could be profound. According to iGaming Business, fewer reporting obligations may make it easier for gaming companies to go public, potentially accelerating Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) and attracting fresh capital.

For African bettors, this translates into a more dynamic market where new operators can enter faster, and existing platforms may benefit from increased liquidity. A study by Statista shows that global online gambling revenue grew by 11% in 2023, and a portion of that growth is being driven by new entrants leveraging relaxed reporting rules.

Prediction Markets Expand into Canada

Kalshi, a U.S.-based prediction market platform, announced a partnership that will bring its services to Canadian customers. The move reflects a broader trend: regulators are beginning to view prediction markets as a legitimate form of wagering rather than a grey‑area activity. Kalshi’s model allows users to bet on outcomes ranging from macro‑economic indicators to sports events, creating a hybrid product that sits between traditional sports betting and financial derivatives.

For African players, Kalshi’s expansion signals that the appetite for alternative betting products is growing worldwide. Operators like Betway and Hollywoodbets have already begun experimenting with similar offerings, such as “skill‑based” e‑sports contests and virtual‑event markets. The diversification of product lines can improve odds competition and give bettors more ways to diversify their stakes.

Responsible Gambling Landscape in Eswatini

A recent high‑profile case in Eswatini resulted in prison sentences for operators running an illegal gambling ring. The case underscores the importance of strong regulatory oversight and the dangers of unlicensed platforms. While the crackdown was severe, it also sparked a conversation about consumer protection across the continent.

Many licensed operators now embed responsible‑gaming tools directly into their apps:

  • Deposit limits – users can set daily, weekly, or monthly caps.
  • Self‑exclusion – a temporary or permanent ban on account access.
  • Reality checks – pop‑up reminders after a set amount of playtime.

Bet9ja, SportyBet, and 1xBet have all rolled out similar features, often in partnership with local NGOs that provide counseling hotlines.

Mobile Betting Penetration in Africa

According to the African Gaming Report 2024, mobile penetration across the continent stands at 68%, with Nigeria, Kenya, and South Africa leading the pack. Mobile‑first betting accounts for 73% of total wagers in these markets, driven by affordable smartphones and widespread mobile money solutions such as M-Pesa and Paystack.

These figures are crucial for operators: a larger mobile audience means higher traffic, but also a greater responsibility to ensure fair play, data security, and responsible gambling.
