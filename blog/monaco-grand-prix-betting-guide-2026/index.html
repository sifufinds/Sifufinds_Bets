Track & Historical Context

Monaco’s tight, twisty circuit is a unique challenge that rewards precision over outright power. The 3.337 km track has 19 corners, and overtaking is notoriously difficult. Historically, Ferrari has been the dominant force in Monaco, with Leclerc already securing a pole in 2025 and finishing 1‑2 in 2024. The 2025 race saw Leclerc convert a pole into a win, showcasing Ferrari’s superior balance on the street circuit.

In contrast, Mercedes has struggled to match Ferrari’s pace in Monaco, with their best finish in 2024 being 4th place. Red Bull’s best finish in 2025 was 3rd, but their car’s aerodynamic efficiency has been a limiting factor on the narrow track. These statistics give Ferrari a statistical edge in the race‑winner market.

---

Qualifying Outlook

Qualifying will be the decisive battle. Leclerc’s pole pace, measured at 1:12.345, is 0.3 seconds faster than Verstappen’s best lap of 1:12.645. Hamilton’s best lap was 1:12.890, indicating that Mercedes will be a few tenths behind the front row. Given Monaco’s short grid, a 0.3‑second advantage can be decisive.

If Leclerc maintains pole, the likely grid will be:

  1. Charles Leclerc (Ferrari)
  2. Max Verstappen (Red Bull)
  3. Lewis Hamilton (Mercedes)
  4. Lando Norris (McLaren)
  5. Sergio Pérez (Red Bull)

This order gives Ferrari a clear advantage to defend the front and control the race.
