Spain sit top of the new FIFA Men's World Ranking published on 21 July 2026, after beating Argentina 1-0 after extra time in the World Cup final. Argentina hold second place, France are third, and Morocco is the highest-ranked African side in eighth. For African bettors, the reshuffle changes the outright markets for the next Africa Cup of Nations cycle and gives early shape to 2027 qualifying odds.

What Changed in the July 2026 FIFA Ranking

FIFA updates its ranking points after every international window, and the World Cup produces the biggest single shift of the four-year cycle. This month's update reflects the full tournament, not just the final.

Spain finished the World Cup as champions and now lead the table with 1,996 points, just ahead of beaten finalists Argentina on 1,970. France sit third, England fourth, and Brazil fifth. For the first time in FIFA ranking history, the top four ranked sides (Spain, Argentina, France and England) were also the four World Cup semi-finalists, which tells you how closely the ranking now tracks actual tournament form.

Europe holds nine of the top fifteen places, a familiar pattern, but the African picture is the one that matters most for readers across Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana and South Africa.

Morocco's eighth place is the country's best-ever ranking position and puts them above sides like Belgium, Germany and Italy. Senegal round out the African representation inside the top 15 at 14th, with both nations now seeded favourably for AFCON qualifying draws and, further down the line, the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations in the group stage pots.

Why the Ranking Matters for Betting Markets

Bookmakers use FIFA ranking movement as one input, alongside squad news and market money, when they set outright odds for AFCON qualifiers and Nations League fixtures. A side jumping several places tends to see its outright winner odds shorten within days of the update, even before a ball is kicked in the new cycle.

Morocco's rise to eighth is already reflected in African bookmaker markets. At the time of writing, Bet9ja and SportyBet both list Morocco among the top three favourites for the next AFCON, alongside Senegal and hosts-to-be-confirmed sides. 1xBet has Morocco's outright AFCON price in the 5.00 to 7.00 range, while Senegal, fresh off their own ranking bump, sits closer to 6.00 to 9.00 depending on the market. Betway's early AFCON qualifying markets have Nigeria and Egypt priced as value outsiders in the 15.00 to 25.00 bracket, reflecting a ranking position outside the African top six.

These prices move fast once qualifying draws are confirmed, so treat any figure here as a snapshot rather than a locked-in number. Always check the live odds on your bookmaker's app before placing a bet.

What This Means for Nigerian, Kenyan and Ghanaian Bettors

A higher FIFA ranking does not guarantee results, but it does shape how bookmakers price a nation relative to its neighbours. Nigerian bettors watching the Super Eagles will notice their side sits outside Africa's top six for now, which keeps outright prices generous but also reflects a genuinely tougher qualifying group on paper. Kenyan and Ghanaian markets follow a similar logic. Ghana's Black Stars and Kenya's Harambee Stars both sit further down the confederation list, so value hunters often find better returns backing them each-way in group markets rather than outright tournament winner bets.

If you are new to reading rankings alongside odds, the simplest approach is this: use the FIFA table to sanity-check whether a bookmaker's price looks fair, then compare that price across two or three platforms before staking anything. Odds on the same fixture can vary by half a point or more between Hollywoodbets, 1xBet and SportyBet, and that gap adds up over a season.
