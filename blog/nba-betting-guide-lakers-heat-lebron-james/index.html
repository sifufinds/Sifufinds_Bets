FAQ

What's the difference between an NBA moneyline and a point spread bet?

A moneyline bet only requires your team to win outright, with favourites priced at shorter decimal odds and underdogs at longer odds. A point spread bet instead handicaps the favourite by a set number of points — a team at -6.5 must win by 7 or more for the bet to pay out, while the underdog can lose by 6 or fewer (or win outright) and still cover.

How do NBA player props work?

Player props let you bet on individual statistics rather than the final score — for example, whether a player scores over or under a set points total, or their combined points, rebounds, and assists. The bookmaker sets a line and you bet whether the player's actual output finishes over or under that number.

What is an NBA totals (over/under) bet?

A totals bet asks you to predict whether the combined final score of both teams will finish higher or lower than the number set by the bookmaker, regardless of which team wins — a popular market for high-scoring matchups involving stars capable of big individual numbers.

Can I bet on NBA games live?

Yes. Live NBA betting lets you wager after tip-off, with spreads, totals, and moneylines updating in real time based on the score and game flow, plus extra markets like which team wins the next quarter.
