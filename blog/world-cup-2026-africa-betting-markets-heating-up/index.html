The 2026 FIFA World Cup has kicked off, and for the first time in the tournament's history, nine African teams are competing on the world stage. CAF's expanded allocation — up from five spots at Qatar 2022 — reflects both Africa's growing football quality and the 48-team format change that opened more doors for the continent.

For African bettors, this creates the richest World Cup betting opportunity in history. Local heroes on the pitch, local bookmakers with competitive odds, and mobile money making deposits faster than ever. Here is everything you need to know.

Africa's 9 Teams at World Cup 2026

Morocco — The Atlas Lions

Africa's highest-ranked side and the undisputed continental favourite. Morocco made history at Qatar 2022 by becoming the first African nation to reach the World Cup semi-finals. Under coach Walid Regragui, they are organised, defensively resilient, and dangerous on the counter-attack. Achraf Hakimi provides world-class quality at right-back and in advanced positions.

WC2026 betting interest: Morocco are the African team with the shortest odds to progress deep into the tournament. Many Nigerian and Ghanaian bettors back Morocco as a "safe" African accumulator pick.

Senegal — The Lions of Teranga

The reigning AFCON champions enter WC2026 as arguably the most complete African squad. Sadio Mané leads a settled group with pace, physicality, and tactical discipline. Senegal are genuinely capable of reaching the Quarter-Finals.

Nigeria — Super Eagles

The biggest betting market on the continent. Every Bet9ja and SportyBet match involving Nigeria sees massive volume from the 60+ million registered bettors in Nigeria alone. The Super Eagles have individual quality — Victor Osimhen, Samuel Chukwueze — but historically struggle for consistency in knockout rounds. Nigerian bettors back the Eagles with heart, often at odds that overestimate emotional value.

Egypt — The Pharaohs

Egypt's campaign is a Mohamed Salah dependency story. With Salah in form, Egypt can cause genuine upsets. Without him, they lack the creative depth to trouble elite opposition. Salah enters this tournament as one of the most-watched players in Africa, and his individual odds markets — first scorer, anytime scorer, Golden Boot — attract significant African bettor volume.

South Africa — Bafana Bafana

South Africa opened the tournament against Mexico — one of the three host nations — in what was arguably the hardest possible group stage draw. Bafana Bafana's return to the World Cup for the first time in over a decade is a cause for celebration, but their chances of advancing from a tough group are rated as low by most bookmakers. The real value play: back South Africa as longshot upsets at 8.00–12.00 per match.

Cameroon — Indomitable Lions

Cameroon's golden generation was led by the Onana era in goal and Song-Billong style physical midfield. Their WC2026 squad carries that combative tradition. They are capable of springing a surprise.

DR Congo, Tunisia, and the remaining CAF qualifiers round out Africa's nine-team contingent — a historic milestone and a testament to the continent's rising global football profile.

Responsible Betting During the World Cup

The World Cup generates enormous betting excitement — but it also generates losses for bettors who don't have a plan. Before placing your first WC2026 bet:

  • Set a total tournament budget. Split it across the group stage, Round of 32, and knockout rounds.
  • Do not place emotional bets on your national team. Nigerian bettors who back the Super Eagles without value assessment typically lose money over the tournament.
  • Use the cash-out feature on Bet9ja and SportyBet if a match turns against you — do not wait for a losing position to expire.
  • If you find yourself chasing losses, stop. The next match is not guaranteed to go your way.
