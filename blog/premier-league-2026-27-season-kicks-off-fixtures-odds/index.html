The Premier League's 2026/27 season is under way. Defending champions Arsenal host newly promoted Coventry City at the Emirates Stadium this evening (Friday 21 August, 19:00 UTC, live on Sky Sports), kicking off a campaign that runs 33 weekends and five midweek match rounds through to Sunday 30 May 2027.

It is a later start than usual. The Premier League pushed the whole calendar back a week this year to give players proper recovery time after the expanded 48-team FIFA World Cup, which finished on 19 July. That leaves 89 clear days between the end of last season and kick-off tonight. Clubs have also been promised at least a 60-hour gap between fixtures over Christmas and New Year to ease the usual festive pile-up, and Boxing Day itself gets a fuller programme than last year, when only one match was shown live. Nine Premier League clubs are competing in UEFA competitions again this season, so expect the usual run of rescheduled kick-offs once European football gets going in September.

Coventry City and Ipswich Town Are Back in the Top Flight

Coventry City arrive in the Premier League for the first time in decades after going up via the Championship, and they could not have been handed a tougher opener than a trip to the champions on the opening night. Ipswich Town's return is the quicker turnaround of the two: they were relegated after the 2024/25 season and are already playing their first game back in the top flight, hosting Sunderland on the opening Saturday. Both clubs will be short-priced outsiders for relegation from day one, but promoted sides have surprised bigger names before, and early-season markets against newly promoted teams are often where value sits before bookmakers adjust their pricing a few games in.

Betting Markets Worth Knowing for Opening Weekend

Outright title odds get the headlines, but most match-day staking on the Premier League happens in smaller markets that update every round:

  • Match result (1X2) — the simplest market, backing a home win, away win or draw for a single fixture.
  • Both teams to score (BTTS) — a good fit for fixtures like Newcastle v Liverpool, where both sides are expected to attack.
  • Over/under goals — total-goals markets tend to price differently for newly promoted sides like Coventry and Ipswich until bookmakers have a few matches of form to work with.
  • Anytime goalscorer — popular for accumulators once the season's early top scorers start to emerge.
  • Live/in-play betting — the market keeps moving after kick-off, which is where a bookmaker's live streaming and cash-out tools actually matter.
