Betting Markets Beyond the Match Winner

Following this story, there are several tennis-specific markets worth knowing beyond simply picking the winner. Set betting lets you predict the exact scoreline in sets — for a best-of-five men's Grand Slam match that could be 3-0, 3-1, 3-2, or the reverse, while women's matches and most ATP/WTA events are best-of-three. The Total Games market sets a line for the combined number of games played across the whole match, letting you bet over or under regardless of who wins — useful in matches expected to go the distance or be tightly contested on serve.

Live, point-by-point betting is widely supported by African bookmakers during Grand Slam fortnight, letting you react to a player losing the opening set, saving break points, or building momentum on serve. FAQ

What is set betting in tennis?

Set betting asks you to predict the exact scoreline in sets, such as 3-0 or 3-1 for a best-of-five men's Grand Slam match, or 2-0/2-1 for a best-of-three women's or ATP 500 match — a higher-risk, higher-reward alternative to simply picking the match winner.

What does the Total Games over/under market mean?

This market sets a line for the combined number of games played across the entire match, and you bet on whether the actual total finishes over or under that number — a good option when you expect a close, extended contest rather than a straightforward win.

How do tennis odds reflect a player's chances?

A shorter decimal price (e.g. 1.40) marks the strong favourite, while a longer price (e.g. 5.00) marks the underdog — the shorter the odds, the higher the bookmaker rates that player's implied chance of winning the match.

Can I bet on tennis matches while they're in progress?

Yes. Live tennis betting updates odds point-by-point and game-by-game, letting you react to shifts in form or momentum as the match unfolds, and is widely supported by licensed African bookmakers throughout Grand Slam tournaments.
